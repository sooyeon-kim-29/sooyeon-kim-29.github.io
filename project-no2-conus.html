Geographic variation in NO₂ exposure, attributable mortality, and sociodemographic differences across urbanicity gradients and regions of the continental US, 2019-2024

The question

Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) comes mostly from traffic and combustion and varies sharply over short distances, so national averages can hide large differences between neighborhoods. How has NO₂ exposure changed across the U.S. in recent years, how many deaths can be attributed to it, and which communities carry the greatest share?

Approach

Using high-resolution land-use regression NO₂ datasets that incorporate satellite observations, we analyze national trends from 2019 to 2024 at the census-tract scale, estimate NO₂-attributable mortality, and assess systematic differences in exposure across sociodemographic characteristics including race/ethnicity, educational attainment, mean income, employment rate, and poverty rate.
