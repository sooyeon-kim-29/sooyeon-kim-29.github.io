Health effects of air-quality regulations in the Seoul Metropolitan Area: a quasi-experimental evaluation

The question

Governments invest heavily in air-quality regulations, but whether those policies translate into measurable health benefits is hard to establish: there is no randomized trial for a city. Did air-quality regulations in the Seoul Metropolitan Area lead to improvements in population health?

Approach

This work, my Master's research at Korea University, applies a quasi-experimental design that combines the synthetic control method with a controlled interrupted time-series analysis. Synthetic control builds a data-driven comparison region from areas not subject to the regulations, giving a counterfactual for what health outcomes would have looked like without the policy.

Why it matters

Quasi-experimental designs offer a way to move from associations toward causal evidence about which environmental policies work, evidence that is directly useful to policymakers.
